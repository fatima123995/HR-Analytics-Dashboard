# HR-Analytics-Dashboard

Problem Statement
Employee attrition is a major challenge for organizations, leading to increased hiring costs and loss of productivity.

This project analyzes HR data to identify:
Which employees are most likely to leave
What factors influence attrition
Where HR should focus retention efforts

Objectives
Measure overall attrition rate
Identify high-risk departments and roles
Analyze attrition by age, tenure, and education
Evaluate whether salary impacts employee retention

Dataset
Source: HR dataset (synthetic / IBM HR Analytics dataset)
Records: ~1,000 employees

Key features:
Age
Department
Job Role
Monthly Income
Years at Company
Attrition (Yes/No)

Tools Used
Microsoft Power BI – Dashboard & data modeling
Microsoft Excel – Data cleaning

Key Metrics
Total Employees: 1,000
Attrition Count: 188
Average Age: 37
Average Income: $6.6K
Early Attrition Rate (<1 Year Employees): 5.2%

Key Insights
1. Department-Level Attrition
R&D shows the highest number of employee exits
→ Indicates potential workload or job-role mismatch
2. Early Attrition (Critical Risk Area)
Employees with <1 year tenure show noticeable attrition
→ Suggests onboarding or expectation gaps
3. Age Group Analysis
Employees aged 25–34 form a major portion of the workforce
→ Retention strategies here would have maximum impact
4. Salary vs Attrition
No strong correlation between salary increase and retention
→ Compensation alone is not enough to reduce attrition

Recommendations
Improve onboarding experience (first 6–12 months)
Conduct exit interviews focused on R&D roles
Develop retention strategies for mid-career employees
Focus on non-monetary factors (growth, culture, management)

Building dashboards is not enough—insights drive value
Defining metrics correctly (e.g., attrition rate vs count) is critical
Data storytelling is essential for decision-making
